# Cue outruns the evidence but mishandles overdue

Cue already implements most of what the research supports for a personal task app, and in several places it goes past anything that has been tested. Its biggest gaps are on the home screen, not in the ranking math. The best-supported principles are a frictionless capture path, a short pre-ranked set instead of the backlog, estimates corrected for the planning fallacy, forgiving lapse handling, cues placed where the action happens, and if-then plans. Of the twelve principles that survive scrutiny, Cue is strong on seven and partial on five. The five partial ones share a cause: work the server does well is not shown on screen. Soft deadlines that have passed get a warning-coloured "Nd overdue" tag and a sixfold ranking boost that never decays. Snoozing an overdue task leaves its due date in place, so it comes back still overdue. The row shows urgency colour but hides the importance the ranking is built on. The "What's in the way?" tools can only be reached through a notification. The phone surfaces show raw estimates while the web shows corrected ones. Cue also leans on weak science in a few places. Its capture rule cites Masicampo and Baumeister (2011), which has never been directly replicated. Its peak-hours multiplier (×1.5 / ×0.6) has no evidence behind its size and, given Nick's hours, probably penalises deep personal work on most weekday evenings. Cue never relies on Zeigarnik or ego depletion, which is to its credit. The highest-value fixes are small UI and parameter changes, not new features.

## Twelve principles survive, and only four carry grade-A evidence

The grading below follows the research notes: A means meta-analytic or preregistered multisite support, B means several primary studies or large field data, C means a single study or contested, and X means failed replication. Ranking combines strength of evidence with expected impact for a single user with depression and variable energy. That second factor is why forgiving lapse handling ranks above if-then plans, even though if-then plans have the stronger meta-analysis.

| Rank | Principle | Grade | Replication status | Expected impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Frictionless capture; structure inferred later, never demanded | B | Consistent across independent field studies (Bellotti 2004, Haraty 2016, Lund & Wiese 2021, Williams & Beale 2025); all small-N and qualitative | High |
| 2 | Show a short pre-ranked set, with the full list one tap away | B | Choice overload holds only under its moderators (Chernev 2015); the mean effect is near zero (Scheibehenne 2010); a reanalysis contests it; never tested on to-do lists | High |
| 3 | Correct duration estimates for the planning fallacy | A (bias) / C (self-correction) | The bias is replicated many times; correcting with one's own history is untested | Medium-high |
| 4 | Forgive lapses: no streak cliffs, no overdue walls | B (streaks, reserves) / C (self-forgiveness, counters) | Seven streak experiments; the reserves effect held in field and lab, with one tiny null replication; no RCT of overdue counters | High for this user |
| 5 | Put the cue at the point of performance, "in the way" | B | Qualitative field data plus notification logs; the CBT RCTs include a planner module but never isolate it | High |
| 6 | If-then plans for tasks that matter | A | 642-test meta-analysis; after bias correction d ≈ 0.15 to 0.35 | Medium |
| 7 | Cut aversive tasks down to a doable first step | A (predictors) / C (intervention) | Steel's predictors are correlational; no RCT of first-step interventions | Medium-high |
| 8 | Retire stale items; "no longer needed" is not failure | B- | A single small study (Bellotti) plus practitioner systems | Medium |
| 9 | Make importance salient at the moment of choice | B | One lab, five experiments; an independent replication was published Sept 2026 but its outcome is unread | Medium |
| 10 | Tie habits to stable cues and judge them by automaticity | B / C | Wood & Neal is well supported; Lally's 66 days comes from one small study | Medium |
| 11 | Give informational feedback, not tangible rewards | A | 128-study meta-analysis; the size of the undermining effect is disputed | Low-medium |
| 12 | Build durable structure and self-experiment rather than escalating nudges | A (method) | Megastudy design compares 54 arms on one population | Medium |

**Capture and choice reduction are the foundation, and they rest on observation rather than experiment.** Bellotti's pilot found an average of **74.4 active to-dos per person spread over 11.25 resources, with only 14% in a list**. Entries were minimal cues, and slow input explained why PDAs were abandoned ([Bellotti et al. 2004](https://hci.rwth-aachen.de/materials/conferences/CHI2004/1p735.pdf)). In Williams and Beale's diary study, **about 88% of tool time went to writing tasks**, and digital-app users rated their productivity no higher than pen-and-paper users (18.36 vs 17.88, p = 0.887) ([Williams & Beale 2025](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2510.06816)). Planning steps that a system prescribes get dropped: **only 2 of 19 graduate students kept up every planning behaviour for five days**, and prioritisation was the first to go ([Lund & Wiese 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10311353)). The case for a short queue is inferential. Chernev's meta-analysis finds overload, and specifically choice deferral, when decisions are hard, preferences are uncertain and the chooser wants to minimise effort ([Chernev et al. 2015](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2015/when-product-assortment-leads-to-choice-overload-a-conceptual)). Scheibehenne's pooled effect is near zero ([Scheibehenne et al. 2010](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/can-there-ever-be-too-many-options-a-meta-analytic-review-of-choice-overload-scheibehenne-benjamin/10008756224)). A low-energy person facing a backlog sits squarely inside Chernev's moderators, so the inference is reasonable, but nobody has compared "one next task" with "full list" directly.

**Lapse handling ranks fourth because the mechanism is well established even though the to-do-specific evidence is thin.** Across seven experiments, a broken streak lowers later engagement, more so when people blame themselves, and less so when the streak can be repaired ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb/full)). Building in emergency reserves raised the share of days on which people met their goal by up to 40% in a step challenge ([Sharif & Shu 2021](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/)). The best gym nudge in the 61,000-person megastudy rewarded **returning after a missed workout**. Only **8% of the 54 interventions still had an effect after the program ended**, and expert forecasters could not tell in advance which ones would work ([Milkman et al. 2021](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/which-programs-give-gym-goers-the-biggest-boost)). The specific claim that overdue piles produce guilt, which then produces avoidance of the app, rests on founder anecdotes ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-think-productivity-apps-accidentally-optimize-for-guilt-retention-a6049f01c8)) and on press coverage of a JPSP study of 28,800 app users. In that study, guilt after a missed deadline predicted abandoning the task ([ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/staying-busy-is-the-secret-to-being-productive-says-study)). Self-forgiveness predicting less procrastination comes from one observational student sample ([BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/cure-procrastination-forgive-yourself)).

**If-then plans have the strongest meta-analysis but a smaller real effect than their reputation.** Across 642 tests, effects were largest for plans in contingent if-then format, made by motivated people, and rehearsed ([Sheeran et al. 2024](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905)). Bias-corrected estimates reported for that review fall to **d = 0.35 (trim-and-fill) and d = 0.15 (robust Bayesian)**, and those figures still need checking against the paper ([summary](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-research/)). The planning fallacy is among the most replicated effects in judgment research. Buehler's thesis writers predicted 34 days and took 55 ([Buehler et al. 1994](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.67.3.366)). Bellotti's professionals matched their predicted durations in only 25% of cases ([Bellotti 2004](https://hci.rwth-aachen.de/materials/conferences/CHI2004/1p735.pdf)).

**Several popular foundations fail or were never tested, and a design should not rest on them.** The Zeigarnik claim that unfinished tasks are better remembered "lacks universal validity". The related tendency to resume interrupted tasks, the Ovsiankina effect, holds up ([Ghibellini & Meier 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)). Masicampo and Baumeister found that making a specific plan quieted intrusive thoughts about unfinished goals ([PubMed 21688924](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21688924/)), but no direct replication exists, and the work comes from the same lab and era as ego depletion. Ego depletion itself failed a 36-lab preregistered replication (**d = 0.06**, Bayes factors favouring the null) ([Vohs et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34520296/)). The GTD weekly review has only a theoretical defence ([Heylighen & Vidal 2008](https://web-archive.southampton.ac.uk/cogprints.org/6289/)). The research turned up no controlled evidence for timeboxing, energy-matched scheduling, or body doubling in adults with ADHD ([arXiv 2509.12153](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12153v1)). The fresh-start effect is observational, and an RCT that used fresh-start framing for medication adherence was null ([Dai et al. 2014](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901)).

## Cue is strong on seven principles and partial on five

Several features go well past what the evidence supports. In most cases that is a deliberate bet, not an error. The predicted-vs-actual mood loop rests on a real forecasting bias. People underpredict how good gratitude, talking to strangers and exercise will feel ([Kumar & Epley 2018](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/people-underestimate-value-sending-letters-appreciation); [Ruby et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021859)). But nobody has tested whether showing people their own forecast errors changes what they do later. The mood tags show only lifters, never drains (`lib/mood.js:235-238`), which is consistent with the emotion-regulation account of procrastination ([Sirois & Pychyl 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/spc3.12011)) but untested as a design rule. The good_days regression and step trials are statistically careful: they report no effects under 10 rated days and require sure ≥ 0.8. Neither has evidence that it changes behaviour. These features cost Nick almost nothing because Claude does the tagging, which sidesteps the overhead complaint Haraty's DIYers raised about GTD ([Haraty et al. 2016](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/edapt/papers/haraty2016_2.pdf)). The real risk is an engineering one: more multipliers make it harder to know why the queue looks the way it does.

## The home screen gets choice and shame mostly right, and overdue and salience wrong

### What's on screen and in what order

The web page runs capture box, header, at most one warning line, Now, Today (X/Y), then later, missed and the Claude drawer (`lib/ui.js:296-297`, `lib/ui.js:420-437`, `lib/ui.js:724-739`). That is close to the textbook answer: one action first, a short committed list, the rest one tap away. The header's single "N hard deadline(s) today" warning, with load, overdue counts and goal gaps moved to the weekly review (`lib/ui.js:421-426`), applies the self-blame findings more faithfully than any commercial app the notes describe. Microsoft's My Day rationale, "a blank slate" against "the anxiety that accompanies an ever-growing to-do list", is marketing rather than a study ([Microsoft 365 blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/blog/2019/09/09/announcing-new-version-microsoft-to-do/)). Cue's locked Today list delivers the same thing, with sizing based on actual pace in place of Sunsama's warning against a threshold ([Sunsama help](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/daily-planning)), and nobody has shown that such warnings change behaviour. Sizing Today to recent output rather than to capacity is the better choice for a user whose capacity varies.

### The ranking: sound skeleton, three weak joints

The score, `(w/p) × (1 + U × P(late if left past today))` with goal boost, nudge and energy fit (`lib/logic.js:358-423`), has the right structure. Importance enters explicitly through area × consequence × magnitude. Urgency is bounded and adds nothing for far-off dates, which is the correct defence against the mere urgency effect ([Zhu, Yang & Hsee 2018](https://econbiz.de/Record/the-mere-urgency-effect-zhu-meng/10011929694)). Bellotti's data agree: importance (81% vs 42% done within a week) mattered and **having a deadline was not significant** ([Bellotti 2004](https://hci.rwth-aachen.de/materials/conferences/CHI2004/1p735.pdf)). The decision to add no aging boost, and instead apply strikes, shrinking and parking, also matches Bellotti's finding that tasks still undone after two weeks are unlikely ever to be done. Boosting old tasks would push exactly those to the top.

The first weak joint is **soft overdue tasks**. They get pLate = 1, which with urgency_soft = 5 makes a **permanent ×6 multiplier** (`lib/logic.js:388-397`). That equals a hard deadline tomorrow carrying 25% risk (1 + 20 × 0.25 = ×6) and is six times what an equally valuable undated task receives. Nothing decays it. A missed soft "do by Tuesday" therefore rides near the top of the ranking, and so in Today, every day until it is done, even though Bellotti found deadlines did not predict completion. This is the "overdue wall" mechanism moved from the header into the ordering.

The second is **energy fit**. Deep tasks get ×1.5 during 09:00-12:00 and ×0.6 outside it (`lib/logic.js:154-166`), a 2.5× swing justified only by an undated reference to May and Hasher. The notes found no evidence for energy-matched scheduling. With Nick's settings, the weekday peak falls inside work hours, where Work-area tasks sort first anyway (`lib/logic.js:168-176`), and his weekday personal hours are 17:00-23:00. A deep personal task, such as a job-search application, is therefore cut to ×0.6 in nearly every weekday window when he could actually do it. Smith's rule already favours short tasks, and the goal boost exists precisely to counter that (`lib/logic.js:254-296`). The energy term pushes in the same direction as Smith's rule, and Marvin's anecdote that every procrastinated task was over 30 minutes suggests long tasks are the ones that need help ([Marvin help](https://help.amazingmarvin.com/en/articles/1949435-will-marvin-fix-my-procrastination-problem)). I rate this possibly counterproductive, not proven harmful, because the goal boost can offset it in areas with an active weekly_min.

The third is **calibration**. Every check-off and reorder is logged "for fitting a paired-comparison model later" (`lib/tools.js:1155-1161`), but no code fits it. The megastudy's lesson that experts cannot forecast which levers work ([Milkman et al. 2021](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/which-programs-give-gym-goers-the-biggest-boost)) applies to hand-set multipliers like PRIORITY_WEIGHT, CONSEQUENCE and the energy factors. Bellotti's central conclusion also bears on this: "the problem with task management is not failure to prioritize well". The ranking formula is more precise than the evidence needs. What matters more is whether the top item is one Nick will actually start.

### Per-row metadata

Each row shows the due tag, mood tag, deep, place, corrected estimate and notes chip. Impact, score and `why` are hidden (`lib/ui.js:716-719`). Showing size is well supported: one of Bellotti's managers found that bucketing tasks into "under 15 min" and "30 min-1 h+" "made her task list seem far less daunting". Hiding impact and showing coloured due tags reproduces, on screen, the conditions of the mere urgency experiments, because the only salient cue at the moment of choice is a deadline. Zhu et al. found the bias **shrank when payoffs were made salient at the moment of choice**. The server already writes the right string ("money, medium, ~25m, due X, 40% risk…", `lib/logic.js:441-462`) and the UI throws it away. One caution applies: the 2026 independent replication's outcome is unknown ([JCR advance access](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/advance-access)), so this should be a cheap change, not a redesign.

### Overdue and stale handling

Stale and parked tasks never appear on the home screen, and expired tasks drop out quietly (`lib/tools.js:742-762`). That is strong. Overdue is the weak spot. Any past due date shows "yesterday" or "Nd overdue" in warning colour whether or not it is hard (`lib/ui.js:379-390`). The row menu's Snooze sets `start`, and "due is untouched" (`lib/ui.js:439-446`). So snoozing an overdue task to Monday brings it back on Monday labelled "6d overdue", with the ×6 boost intact. The JPSP app-log finding was that frictionless rescheduling, not missing a deadline, separated people who eventually finished from people who abandoned in guilt ([ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/staying-busy-is-the-secret-to-being-productive-says-study)). The notes recommend marking only real external deadlines as failed and letting soft intentions roll over ([Omni forum](https://discourse.omnigroup.com/t/due-versus-defer-best-practice/59332)). Cue's data model already separates `hard`; the display and ranking just ignore it for overdue tasks. The weekly review triages overdue items, but that is up to seven days of a red label first.

### Empty states and completion

Completion copy is informational and self-compassionate: "Today's list is done. You showed up for it." and "You did the one thing. That's today handled." (`lib/ui.js:714-723`). This fits the finding that positive feedback raised free-choice behaviour (d = 0.33) while tangible rewards undermined it ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://home.ubalt.edu/ntygmitc/642/Articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf)). The empty state, "Nothing waiting on you right now", is neutral but inert. Its wording differs on every surface: "Nothing up next" on Android and "Nothing up / Capture something" on the new tab (`lib/ui.js:721`, `NowWidget.kt:64`, `chrome/newtab.js:15`). It also ignores the server's `needs_next_action` goal flag (`lib/logic.js:254-296`), which marks exactly the case where an empty queue hides a stalled goal.

### One-tap actions

One-tap check-off with a 15-minute undo and an optional one-tap mood ask (`lib/ui.js:701-705`, `lib/ui.js:901-917`) is right: 11 of 19 of Lund and Wiese's participants said checking off was motivating. Done, Start 25 and Not now on every Now surface put the action at the point of performance. Fixed-interval timers have one supporting randomised study, a single day with 87 students ([Biwer et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36859717/)). The missing one-tap action is the one aimed at the actual mechanism of procrastination. Steel's meta-analysis names task aversiveness and low self-efficacy as core predictors ([Steel 2007](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/handle/1880/47914)), and "What's in the way?" targets both. Yet it opens only on the next visit after Not now on a notification (`lib/ui.js:619-640`). The row's "•••" menu offers Focus, Move, Snooze and Drop, but not "Too big" or "Don't know how to start". The shrink_due flag for an obligation that has hit three strikes reaches Claude (`lib/tools.js:1015`) but never the row.

### Cross-surface consistency

The rule of one next action, then a short Today list, holds everywhere. That is a real achievement given five surfaces. The details drift. Estimates are corrected on the web but raw everywhere else (`lib/push.js:49`, `NowWidget.kt:14-104`), which means the glance surfaces understate durations by about the 1.3× prior the server itself assumes. The launcher's "Also today" rows can be checked off in one tap and open `/?task=id` (`HomeActivity.kt:358-391`). The widget's rows cannot be checked off and open plain `/` (`NowWidget.kt:60-100`). The list length varies too: up to 5 on the widget, 3 on the launcher and compact card, and 1-5 on the web. Each choice is defensible on its own, but the same task shows different minutes depending on where Nick looks, and that erodes trust in the corrected estimate, which is the feature with the strongest evidence behind it.

## Cue leans on one unreplicated finding and one unsupported multiplier

**Masicampo and Baumeister is load-bearing only in the text, not in the behaviour.** The capture instruction justifies verb-first next actions with "what stops an open loop from nagging (Masicampo & Baumeister 2011)" (`lib/tools.js:28`). The behaviour is right for better-supported reasons. Bellotti shows minimal cues go stale. The resumption tendency (Ovsiankina) is real. Microsoft's microtask work exists because many list items are not actionable ([White et al. 2021](https://www.academia.edu/91876723/Microtask_Detection)). The open-loop story should be dropped from the citation, because Claude reads these instructions and repeats them. A grade C rationale should not travel as fact. The same paper warns that very specific plans can make people closed-minded about other routes to a goal ([Masicampo & Baumeister](https://typeset.io/pdf/committed-but-closed-minded-when-making-a-specific-plan-for-7mwopcdiw8.pdf)), which is an argument for keeping plans easy to re-slot.

**Cue avoids the other failed findings.** No mechanism invokes Zeigarnik memory tension. The low-day trigger ("nothing done by 11:00" or mood ≤ 4) reads state from data rather than claiming that willpower drains with use, so ego depletion is not at work. The routine card's "d of n steps" progress bar is a legitimate use of the resumption tendency and the goal gradient ([Kivetz et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39)). The weak spot is the peak-hours multiplier discussed above: the synchrony effect was not assessed in this research, and nothing supports a 2.5× swing. Several citations in Cue, including Harkin 2016, Kluger and DeNisi 1996, Stawarz 2015 and Leroy 2009, were outside the scope of these notes and are neither confirmed nor refuted here. The low-day design invokes behavioural activation and graded task assignment, and the notes did not search that literature either. Duolingo's lowered minimum, where one lesson keeps the streak, raised the share of learners holding a 7+ day streak from about a third to just over half ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)), which is industry evidence that a "minimum viable day" improves consistency.

## Eight changes, ranked by value per hour of work

The first change is to **fix soft overdue tasks**, both on the row and in the ranking. Show "Nd overdue" in warning colour only when `hard` is true, and show a neutral "from Tue" for soft dates. Make Snooze on a task whose due date has passed move `due` as well as `start`, or add a one-tap "New date" that does. Cap or decay the soft-overdue urgency term, for example by letting pLate fall back toward the undated level after a few days and routing the task to a re-date prompt (`lib/ui.js:379-390`, `lib/ui.js:439-446`, `lib/logic.js:388-397`). This removes the only failure signal left on the home screen and the permanent ×6 that creates an overdue wall in Today. The case rests on the guilt and self-blame findings plus Bellotti's null result for deadlines.

Second, **put "What's in the way?" on the row**. Add "Too big / Don't know how to start / Not feeling it" to the task "•••" menu, and show a small "make it smaller" chip on rows flagged shrink_due (`lib/ui.js:439-446`, `lib/ui.js:619-640`, `lib/tools.js:1015`). This targets the strongest procrastination predictors directly, and the code already exists.

Third, **send est_corrected to every surface**: push, widget, launcher and new tab (`lib/push.js:49`, `api/push.js:25-26`). The planning fallacy is Cue's best-evidenced correction, and it currently stops at the web page.

Fourth, **show one short importance cue**. Render the existing `why` when a row is expanded, and add a one-word consequence tag (money, health, legal, career) for obligations with magnitude ≥ 2 (`lib/logic.js:441-462`, `lib/ui.js:716-719`). This rebalances an on-screen signal that today shows only urgency. Keep it off low days, so it doesn't read as pressure.

Fifth, **shrink the energy-fit multipliers** to something like ×1.15 / ×0.9. Alternatively, apply the deep-task penalty only within personal hours relative to a personal-time peak, so evening deep work isn't taxed by a peak that falls inside work hours (`lib/logic.js:154-166`). Then check the result against the choice log.

Sixth, **use the choice log before adding any more multipliers**. A simple first step is a weekly "picked something other than #1" rate and the rank of the chosen task, reported in how_cue_is_working (`lib/tools.js:1155-1161`, `lib/tools.js:2120`). If Nick routinely picks rank 4, the weights are wrong in a way the formula can't reveal.

Seventh, **add an optional "when" to tasks Nick puts off or flags as important, and rehearse it back**. Show "Tue 9:00 → open draft" on the row and in the Now notification, echoing how habit cards already show their if-then line. Ask for it only on tasks with strikes or high impact, because the effect depends on motivation and asking on every capture would add the friction Lund and Wiese saw abandoned. Expect a small effect (d ≈ 0.15 to 0.35).

Eighth, **unify the small cross-surface details**. Make widget Today rows checkable and open `/?task=id` as the launcher's do. Use one empty-state line everywhere. When the queue is empty but a goal has `needs_next_action`, offer "Pick a next step for <goal>" rather than "Nothing waiting on you".

## Conclusion

The research does not argue for more features in Cue. It argues for exposing what the server already computes and removing the last places where the app's own mechanics recreate the failure pile it was designed to avoid. The most important insight is that Cue's overdue problem does not live in the header, which it already fixed, but in two quieter places: a ranking term that never forgives a missed soft date, and a Snooze that does not actually reschedule. Both are cheap to fix and both sit on the user's actual path through the app.

The second implication concerns method. The most reliable lesson from the megastudy is that nobody, including the people who design nudges, can predict which levers will work. Cue is unusual in already having the instrumentation to answer that for one person: the micro-randomised idle trial, step trials, and a paired-comparison choice log. The next gains in the queue are more likely to come from fitting the ranking to Nick's own choices than from adding another literature-derived multiplier, especially when the literature behind several of the current ones turns out to be thinner than the code comments suggest.
